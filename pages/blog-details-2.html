The Blueprint of RO Membranes

Why household inlet pressure directly dictates the filtration life of thin-film composite membranes.

August 2026 7 min read
At a Glance

Membrane performance metrics

Your RO membrane is the most critical — and most sensitive — component in the entire filtration chain.

TFCthin-film composite material

What is a Thin-Film Composite Membrane?

Modern household RO membranes are made from Thin-Film Composite (TFC) material — an ultra-thin polyamide active layer (0.2 microns) bonded onto a porous polysulfone support. This layered architecture allows water molecules to pass while blocking dissolved salts, heavy metals, bacteria, and organic compounds.

How Osmosis Works

Water is pushed by pump pressure across the membrane from high-concentration to low-concentration side. Dissolved solids too large to pass are diverted to the drain as reject water.

Pressure: The Silent Membrane Killer

The membrane operates within a precise pressure window of 40–80 PSI. Below this threshold, insufficient water crosses the membrane, increasing contact time of reject water and accelerating scaling deposits on the membrane surface. Above 80 PSI, mechanical stress degrades the polyamide layer, creating micro-tears that reduce rejection rates.

Homes with municipal supply pressure below 35 PSI must use a booster pump. Without one, the membrane doesn't fully function — you get a slow fill, poor TDS rejection, and a membrane that ages prematurely from constant low-pressure fouling.

The 3 Stages That Protect the Membrane

A healthy RO membrane depends on clean pre-treatment. Three filtration stages must do their job before water reaches the membrane:

  1. Sediment Filter (5 micron) — removes sand, silt, and rust particles
  2. Pre-Carbon Block — removes chlorine which destroys the polyamide layer
  3. 5-Micron Carbon Filter — removes residual organics and chloramines

Signs Your Membrane is Failing

A failing membrane has a gradual, hard-to-notice signature. Watch for these signs:

  • Output TDS rising above 20–25% of inlet TDS
  • Tank fills in more than 4 hours consistently
  • Significantly more reject water than usual
  • Taste becomes slightly salty or flat despite clean pre-filters
Key Takeaways

Protect your membrane

Check inlet pressure

Buy a pressure gauge (₹150) and verify your inlet is 40–80 PSI. Install a booster pump if below 35 PSI.

Replace pre-filters on time

A clogged sediment or carbon filter lets chlorine and particles reach the membrane, degrading it fast.

Monitor TDS monthly

A rising output TDS trend is the earliest warning of membrane degradation. Act before water quality drops noticeably.
