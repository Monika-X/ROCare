Understanding TDS Levels in Drinking Water

What do parts per million actually mean for your health, tea extraction, and daily taste profiles?

What is TDS?

TDS stands for Total Dissolved Solids — the combined measurement of all inorganic and organic substances dissolved in water. This includes minerals like calcium, magnesium, sodium, potassium, chlorides, sulfates, and bicarbonates. It is measured in milligrams per litre (mg/L), which is equivalent to parts per million (ppm).

Quick Definition

1 ppm = 1 mg of dissolved solids per litre of water. A TDS meter measures the electrical conductivity of water to estimate this value.

Why TDS Levels Matter for Health

Very low TDS water (below 50 ppm) is essentially demineralised. While it is free of harmful contaminants, it can leach minerals from the body and has a flat, unappealing taste. Very high TDS water (above 500 ppm) may contain harmful concentrations of heavy metals, nitrates, or industrial pollutants.

The Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) recommends a TDS limit of 500 ppm for drinking water, with a desirable range of 100–300 ppm for the best taste and mineral balance. A quality RO purifier with a remineralizer brings water into this sweet spot.

TDS and Your Morning Tea

Water chemistry dramatically affects tea and coffee extraction. At 75–150 ppm, the mineral balance allows polyphenols and aromatic compounds to dissolve efficiently, producing a balanced, full-flavoured cup. At very low TDS (below 50 ppm), tea tastes thin and sour. At high TDS (above 300 ppm), tannins bind to calcium ions, creating a chalky, astringent finish.

Low

<50 ppm — Flat, thin taste

Ideal

How ROCare Optimises Your TDS

A properly maintained RO system reduces TDS to near-zero at the membrane stage, then a remineralizer cartridge adds back precise amounts of calcium and magnesium to reach the ideal 75–150 ppm range. This is why post-service water from ROCare visits often tastes noticeably crisper.

Checking TDS quarterly is a simple way to know if your membrane needs attention — if output TDS rises above 20–25% of input TDS, it signals membrane degradation.

Key Takeaways

What to remember

Test regularly

A pocket TDS meter costs ₹300–600 and takes 10 seconds to give a reading. Do it quarterly.

Remineralise

Don't drink zero-TDS water. Ensure your purifier has a working remineralizer stage.

Rising TDS = membrane alert

If output TDS creeps up month over month, it signals your membrane is losing efficiency.
