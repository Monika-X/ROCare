TDS 101: What 60 vs 650 ppm Means

The single metric that decides which filter your home needs — explained with pressure and taste context.

August 2026 5 min read
At a Glance

60 vs 650: two waters, two filters

60 ppm needs taste polishing. 650 ppm needs heavy RO. TDS tells you which.

60 ppmmunicipal • taste fix
650 ppmborewell • RO needed
75-150ideal drinking TDS

The single metric that decides

60 ppm municipal water is soft, low-mineral — chlorine and taste are the issue, not hardness. 650 ppm borewell water is hard, mineral-heavy — heavy metals and scaling are the real risk. One needs carbon polishing, the other needs RO. A TDS meter tells you in 10 seconds.

Pressure + Taste Context

Low TDS water often has low pressure needs and flat taste; high TDS water needs higher pressure for RO and tastes chalky. Both affect extraction for tea/coffee.

What to do at 60 vs 650

  • At 60 ppm: Carbon + UV is enough; RO would over-strip.
  • At 650 ppm: RO + remineralizer is mandatory; carbon alone won't help.
  • Check quarterly: If output rises >25% of input, membrane is fading.
Key Takeaways

What to remember

Test quarterly

₹300 meter, 10 seconds — know if you need carbon or RO.

60 = carbon, 650 = RO

Don't over-filter soft water; don't under-filter hard water.

Taste follows TDS

75-150 ppm gives crisp, balanced tea and drinking taste.
