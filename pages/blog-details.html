When should RO filters be replaced?

Filter life depends on water quality, usage, and service discipline. Here is a practical way to know when your purifier needs attention.

Overview

Do not wait for bad taste alone

Taste is useful, but flow, pump cycling, tank fill time, and TDS trend give a more complete view.

Highlights

What this page helps you do

Time-based signs

Pre-filters often need replacement before the main membrane does.

Flow-based signs

Slow filling can point to clogged filters, low pressure, or membrane stress.

Quality checks

TDS trend and odor checks help separate filter issues from source-water changes.

Process

A practical replacement rhythm

Use service dates as a guide, then adjust based on water source, family size, and measured performance.

  1. Record the last service date.
  2. Compare current flow and taste against normal use.
  3. Replace pre-filters before they overload the membrane.

Quick takeaways

Low flow

Usually starts with clogged filters or pressure problems.

Odd taste

Often points to carbon, post-carbon, or storage hygiene.

High TDS

Needs careful membrane and system inspection.
